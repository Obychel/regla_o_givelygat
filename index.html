Overview of the Conlang "Regla o Givelygan"

This document describes the alphabet, phonetics, core grammatical features, and lexicon of the constructed language "Regla o Givelygan."

0. Lexicon (Root List)

The full root-list lexicon is maintained as a separate online document, kept up to date independently of this page:

Regla o Givelygat — Lexicon (Google Docs)

1. Alphabet and Phonetics

The language uses the Latin alphabet, with the following features:

  • Alphabet inventory: The letters b, h, p, q are absent.
  • Vowels: y is pronounced [ʉ]. There are no diphthongs.
  • Consonants: c — [t͡s], g — [ɣ], j — [j]. The only voiced stop is d [d].
  • Allophony:
    • n /n/ → [ŋ] /_k; [ɰ̃] /_g
    • m /m/ → [ɱ/m̪] /_{f, v}
    • v /v/ → [β̞] /V_C
  • Phonotactics and palatalization:
    • tj [tɕ]
    • sj [ɕ]
    • xj [kɕ]
    • dj [dʑ]
    • zj [ʑ]
  • Stress: Free, with a tendency to fall on the syllable preceding the ending.
  • Phonotactics: No more than two consonants may cluster together. Clusters of the type "nasal/sonorant + fricative/stop" are prohibited word-initially.
  • Adaptation of loanwords to the language's phonetics:
    • b / w → v
    • p → f
    • h → g
    • q → k
    • Vo → Vwo
    • Vu → Vw / Vwu

2. Key Principle: Affix Harmony

The basis of the morphology is a rule of vowel/consonant alternation at morpheme boundaries:

  • A consonant-final stem takes a vowel-initial affix.
  • A vowel-final stem takes a consonant-initial affix.

3. Morphology

Typologically, the morphology of Regla o Givelygan is agglutinative with elements of analyticity.

  • Agglutinative feature: Stems can take TMVA suffixes to form verbal stems, after which terminal endings are added to verbal or non-verbal stems to derive specific word classes.
  • Analytic feature: Many grammatical meanings (gender, reality/abstractness, degrees of comparison, negation) are expressed by function words or constructions.

3.1. Stem Types and the Formation of Word Classes

There are two types of stems:

  1. Non-Verbal (Primary/Basic) Stem (BS): Used to form nominal word classes, adverbs, and adjectives directly.
    • May be a root (which is itself a BS).
    • May be formed from a root or another BS by means of derivational suffixes or derivational prefixes:
      • Instrumental suffix: -ll- (after a vowel), -orl- (after a consonant).
        Examples:
        • tavalla (n. oven) = tava- + -ll- + -a (as the instrument of the action, contrasting with tavas — n. bread = tava- + -s, the result of the action);
        • deflorl- (stem: n. world, universe; adj. worldly, universal) = defl- + -orl-;
        • fonorl- (stem: vb. to light/ignite, n. lighter);
        • fijorl- (stem: vb. to switch on a device, n. device/appliance);
        • alella (n. light, lamp);
        • fevull- (stem: n. seed, sperm; vb. to form seed, to ejaculate);
        • ivoll- (stem: n. genital organ, vb.tr. to have intercourse with);
        • rojilla (n. receptacle, sack);
        • jadorla (n. buttocks);
        • galulla (n. breast/chest);
        • jerell- (stem: n. circle, vb. to become round/curve);
        • loforla (n., physics: matter);
        • mynella (n. nose).
      • Occupational suffix: -ler- (after a vowel), -ajo- (after a consonant).
      • Comitative suffix: -nyk-/-ng- (after a vowel), -elo- (after a consonant).
      • Possessive suffix: -no...- (after a vowel), -on...- (after a consonant). Derived from the preposition on, with the forms:
        • After a vowel: 1sg: -nos- (← on osa), 1pl (ex): -nost- (← on osut), 1pl (in): -nosm- (← on osima), 2sg: -nor- (← on ara), 2pl: -nors- (← on arus), 3sg: -gos- (← o ges), 3pl: -got- (← o getu).
        • After a consonant: 1sg: -ons- (← on osa), 1pl (ex): -ont- (← *onst ← on osut), 1pl (in): -omm- (← *onm ← *onsm ← on osima), 2sg: -onr- (← on ara), 2pl: -onzi- (← *onrzi ← on aruzi), 3sg: -ogs-/-ox- (← *ogs ← o ges), 3pl: -ogtu-/-oktu- (← *ogtu ← o getu).
      • Antonymic prefix (reverses meaning): mi- (for consonant-initial stems) / mij- (for vowel-initial stems).
        Examples: nidar (adj. simple) = nida- + -r, and minidar (adj. complex) = mi- + nida- + -r; mer- (stem: vb. to live, n. life) and mimer- (stem: vb. to die, n. death); fijorl- (stem: vb. to switch on a device, n. device/appliance) and mifijorl- (stem: vb. to switch off a device); mijali- (stem: vb. to lose consciousness, n. the unconscious, adj. unconscious); mic- (stem: vb. to be absent, n. absence); mivis- (stem: vb. to end/finish, n. the end, adj. last/final); mijorv- (stem: vb. to uncover, to discover); misamr- (stem: adj. quiet, adv. quietly, n. silence).
  2. Verbal Stem (VS): Formed by adding a TMVA suffix to any other stem (root, BS). Used to form finite verbs, action nouns, participles, and converbs.

    When a VS is derived from a BS denoting a property, different constructions are used to distinguish the action of entering a state from the action of the property's intensification:

    • d- + noun/adjective from the BS (action of state)
    • verb/participle/converb from the VS (action of the property's progression)

Morphology of the Core Word Classes:

  • Noun (+ pronoun, numeral): BS + Noun-class ending
  • Adjective: BS + Attributive-class ending
  • Adverb: BS + Adverbial ending (-ov / -go)
  • Verb (finite): VS + EPN ending
    • The verb d- (to be) is an exception: Truncated tense suffix, without voice, + EPN ending (the initial da- is dropped). Historical examples: *danajs → najs, *darejko → rejko, *dakart → kart, *dadara → dara, *dajysi → jysi, dane(l) → ne(l) ("being," attributive form), dano → no ("being," adverbial form).
  • Action noun: VS + Noun-class ending
  • Participle: VS + Attributive-class ending
  • Converb: VS + Adverbial ending (-ov / -go)
  • Prepositions, particles, conjunctions: Have a simple, monomorphemic form.

A. Verbalization (Formation of the Verbal Stem): TMVA Suffixes

Converts any stem into a Verbal Stem (VS).

Tense/Mood/Voice/Aspect (TMVA) suffixes:

  • Imperfective aspect:
    • For consonant-final stems (require a vowel-initial suffix):
      • Present Indicative: Active -en-, Reflexive -an-, Passive -in-, Reciprocal -un-.
      • Past Indicative: Active -er-, Reflexive -ar-, Passive -ir-, Reciprocal -ur-.
      • Future Indicative: Active -ek-, Reflexive -ak-, Passive -ik-, Reciprocal -uk-.
      • Imperative: Active -ed-, Reflexive -ad-, Passive -id-, Reciprocal -ud-.
      • Atemporal (general time): Active -ej-, Reflexive -aj-, Passive -ij-, Reciprocal -uj-.
    • For vowel-final stems (require a consonant-initial suffix):
      • Present Indicative: Active -ne-, Reflexive -na-, Passive -ni-, Reciprocal -nu-.
      • Past Indicative: Active -re-, Reflexive -ra-, Passive -ri-, Reciprocal -ru-.
      • Future Indicative: Active -ke-, Reflexive -ka-, Passive -ki-, Reciprocal -ku-.
      • Imperative: Active -de-, Reflexive -da-, Passive -di-, Reciprocal -du-.
      • Atemporal (general time): Active -je-, Reflexive -ja-, Passive -ji-, Reciprocal -ju-.
  • Perfective aspect:
    • For consonant-final stems (require a vowel-initial suffix):
      • Past Indicative: Active -evr-, Reflexive -avr-, Passive -ivr-, Reciprocal -uvr-.
      • Future Indicative: Active -evk-, Reflexive -avk-, Passive -ivk-, Reciprocal -uvk-.
      • Imperative: Active -evd-, Reflexive -avd-, Passive -ivd-, Reciprocal -uvd-.
      • Atemporal (general time): Active -ev-, Reflexive -av-, Passive -iv-, Reciprocal -uv-.
    • For vowel-final stems (require a consonant-initial suffix):
      • Past Indicative: Active -rve-, Reflexive -rva-, Passive -rvi-, Reciprocal -rvu-.
      • Future Indicative: Active -kve-, Reflexive -kva-, Passive -kvi-, Reciprocal -kvu-.
      • Imperative: Active -dve-, Reflexive -dva-, Passive -dvi-, Reciprocal -dvu-.
      • Atemporal (general time): Active -ve-, Reflexive -va-, Passive -vi-, Reciprocal -vu-.

Voices:

  • Meaning of the voices for transitive verbs:
    • Active: action of the subject upon an external object (yn).
    • Reflexive: action of the subject upon itself.
    • Passive: action upon the subject from an external source; the agent is marked with the preposition gyl.
    • Reciprocal: action of two subjects upon each other.
  • Voice peculiarities for intransitive actions:
    • Reflexive: the default voice for a simple action ("to go").
    • Active: adds a meaning of coercion (to make someone go).

3.2. Terminal Endings for Word-Form Formation

A single terminal ending is attached to the stem (BS or VS).

A. Predicates (Finite Verbs)

VS + EPN ending (Evidentiality, Person, Number) / Conditional ending

  • EPN endings (after a consonant sound of the VS):
    • 1sg: -ajs (direct), -ukes (hearsay), -oc (assumptive), -esim (probabilitive).
    • 2sg: -or (direct), -erku (hearsay), -art (assumptive), -orim (probabilitive).
    • 3sg: -oj (direct), -ejko (hearsay), -ati (assumptive), -amy (probabilitive).
    • 1pl: -ysi (direct), -usk (hearsay), -ust (assumptive), -omze (probabilitive).
    • 2pl: -ara (direct), -okri (hearsay), -odre (assumptive), -erme (probabilitive).
    • 3pl: -aje (direct), -ejki (hearsay), -ejde (assumptive), -ojmu (probabilitive).
  • EPN endings (after a vowel sound of the VS):
    • 1sg: -zy / -sy (direct), -skej (hearsay), -ci (assumptive), -smy (probabilitive).
    • 2sg: -re (direct), -rka (hearsay), -rto (assumptive), -ram (probabilitive).
    • 3sg: -xa (direct), -xaj (hearsay), -xet (assumptive), -xom (probabilitive).
    • 1pl: -saj (direct), -zke (hearsay), -staj (assumptive), -zma (probabilitive).
    • 2pl: -roj (direct), -kor (hearsay), -dar (assumptive), -mur (probabilitive).
    • 3pl: -ji / -je (direct), -jka (hearsay), -dje (assumptive), -jme (probabilitive).
  • Conditional endings (after a vowel): -elsy (1sg), -arel (2sg), -olaj (3sg), -olas (1pl), -elyr (2pl), -yjyl (3pl).
  • Conditional endings (after a consonant): -zla (1sg), -rali (2sg), -leja (3sg), -lezi (1pl), -rel (2pl), -ljo (3pl).

D. Adverbs and Converbs

BS (for adverbs) or VS (for converbs) + adverbial ending (-ov / -go)

Step-by-step example: "knowing, in general"
  • Step 1 (Forming the VS): lare- (BS, idea: to know) + -je- (TMVA suffix, Atemporal Active) → lareje- (VS, "to know [in general]").
  • Step 2 (Forming the Participle): lareje- (VS) + -r (Attributive-class ending, Class I, singular, focus) → larejer ("knowing, in general").

4. Syntax

  • Word order: Basic S dO V iO, where dO is the direct object, marked with the preposition yn, and iO is the indirect object, marked with any preposition other than yn. Within phrases: Head-Modifier.
  • Verb valency:
    • Intransitive/monovalent verbs (fyla- "to sleep") combine only with a subject.
    • Monotransitive/divalent verbs (okaj- "to create") govern a direct object (yn).
    • Ditransitive/trivalent verbs (iraj- "to give") govern a direct object (yn) and either a recipient indirect object (re) or a source indirect object (ly/l').
    • The intransitive, mono- or divalent verbs d- (to be) and s- (not to be) combine, on their left, with the subject, and on their right, with the nominal part of the predicate (the predicative), likewise in the nominative case.

4.1. Complex (Subordinate) Sentences

Relative clauses ("which/that") are absent; their meaning is conveyed through other constructions (e.g., participial phrases).

  • Cause ("because"): mir + action noun (focus). The subject of the action noun is marked with the preposition o/on.
    Example: mir regelevra on osa yn dus dyl lal (because I put it there; lit. "because of my placing this there").
  • Purpose ("in order to"): fi / f' + action noun in the atemporal.
    Example: fi larejes on ara (so that you would know; lit. "for your knowing").
  • Intention (about to do something): gal- (to head toward a goal) + dyl + action noun in the atemporal.
    Example: galanejki dyl vajaval (I heard they are about to come; lit. "they go [by hearsay] toward the coming").
  • Result ("so that"): mir + action noun (topic). The construction is nearly the same as for cause.
  • Concession ("although"): converb togeno (having) + action noun in the accusative (yn).
    Example: genyrvexa togeno yn larenezi (he asked, although he knew; lit. "he asked, having knowledge").
  • Coercion (Causative): The Active voice is preferred for intransitive verbs. Alternatively: lene- (to do) + yn + action noun in the atemporal + o/on + a nominal in the nominative; the yn + action-noun part and the o/on + nominal part are both optional.
    Example: gityjare so re leneves yn jonifeja on osa yn ges (gity-ja-re + so + re + lene-ve-s + yn + jonif-ej-a + on + os-a + yn + ge-s) — you cannot (in general) make me love him.
  • Condition ("if"):
    1. Analytically: son + action noun.
      Example: nadakezy son genykeze on ara (I will answer if you ask).
    2. Synthetically: The verb in the conditional clause takes special conditional endings instead of EPN endings.
      Example: genykerali, nadakezy (if you ask, I will answer).

4.2. Degrees of Comparison and Pragmatics

  • Degrees of comparison (Analytic):
    • Comparative degree (+): [Attribute] + gax + ja/j' + [compared item] (optional).
      Example: danoj re rogari gax ja lal (it is redder than that).
    • Comparative degree (−): [Attribute] + lez + ja/j' + [compared item] (optional).
      Example: danaje roganu lez (they are less red).
    • Superlative degree ("the most..."): [Attribute] + rydo ("to the limit").
      Example: rogar rydo (the most red / reddest). Examples of negation with differing emphasis: sane rogar rydo (the least red; literally "not-red to the limit") versus rogar rydo so (not the most red).
  • Politeness: No grammatical distinctions exist.
  • Formality: Expressed through the choice of more elaborate syntactic constructions.
  • Emphatic particle: ka (comparable to "indeed," "you know") is placed after the word being emphasized.

5. Negation

  • Colloquial/vernacular register (analytic, with a negative adverb from the stem s-, after words denoting an action): [positive form of the verb/participle/converb/action noun] + so (s- + -o). Emphasizes the absence of some action by someone/something.
    Example: larejerto so (I suppose you don't know).
  • Informal/conversational register (analytic, with a participle from the stem s-, after words denoting the name or a property of a thing or action): sane/sare/sake + [positive form of the noun/adjective/participle/action noun] (sane=s-+-an-+-e, sare=s-+-ar-+-e, sake=s-+-ak-+-e). Emphasizes that something bears no direct relation to a given name/property of a thing or action; functions as an ordinary or substantivized attributive.
    Example: sanel roga ("not red" — the original Russian gloss puns on the archaic sense of krasnyj, "beautiful"), sane dronal ("not a stone").
  • Elevated / embellished register (analytic, with a verb from the stem s-): [default present-tense form of s-] + [participial phrase]. Functions as the predicate: the positive predicate turns into a participial phrase, in which the positive verb (other than "to be" from the stem d-) becomes a participle; in a phrase built on the verb from stem d-, that verb is replaced by the corresponding verb from stem s-.
    Example: sanoj larekveri (he won't find out; lit. "he does not become knowing"), sanoj rogari (he is not red).
  • Determiner pronoun "no/none": s- + Attributive-class ending (according to the stem type).
    Example: se (none, singular, focus).

6. Numerals

  • Basic roots: Nyl- (0), Est- (1), Lar- (2), Gid- (3), Nav- (4), Mir- (5), Av- (6), Vyz- (7), Sed- (8), Ig- (9), Lam- (10).
  • Multiplicative and additive suffixes: -elmor-/-elm- (+10), -ard- (×10), -eg- (×100), -ynd- (×1000).
  • Large numbers: Ciz- (10,000), Miljon- (1,000,000).
  • Power suffix (104n): -ol-.
    Examples:
    • estola jon ciza norujaje — 10,000 (synonym 1) and 10,000 (synonym 2) are equal to each other;
    • cizola joj ulel gaxo — 1040,000 is a very great many;
    • larardola mirolel — 104×20 × 104×5 = 10100 (a googol).
  • Compound numerals: Built on the pattern [form of the VS of order_1] + [form of the BS of order_2] + [form of the BS of order_3] + ..., with every order after the first taking the BS form. Tens, hundreds, and thousands are each expressed synthetically, while larger orders (multiples of four) are expressed analytically through a quantitative-nominal construction — roughly "X times 104n," where X follows 104n and takes the attributive (BS) form.
    Examples:
    • 173 (topic): Gidaja vyzelmarda (gid-aj-a + vyz-elm-ard-a)
    • 101010 (focus): lamajal estynda ciza lame (lam-aj-al + est-ynd-a + ciz-a + lam-e)
  • Cardinal numerals:
    • If it modifies a quantity of an order not a multiple of four: Stem + (TMVA reflexive-voice suffix) + Attributive-class ending.
    • If it modifies a quantity of an order that is a multiple of four: Stem + Attributive-class ending.
    Example: Drona estelmajel estelmege cize estelme estelmege (dron-a + est-elm-aj-el + est-elm-eg-e + ciz-e + est-elm-e + est-elm-eg-e) — 11,111,111 stones, focus; literally "stone × (11 + 11×100 + 10,000×(11 + 11×100))."
  • Ordinal numerals: Numeral stem + (TMVA passive-voice suffix) + Attributive-class ending.
    Example: 91st (focus): Estijel igarde (Est-ij-el + ig-ard-e).
  • Numeral adverbs:
    • Numeral stem + -an- + -o → meaning "n times." Example: vyzano (seven times).
    • Numeral stem + -in- + -o → meaning "every nth time." Example: mirino (every fifth time).

8. Clarifications and Examples

  • Reality/Abstractness (Analytic):
    • uva drone (uv- + -a [Attributive-class ending] + dron- + -e [Noun-class ending]) = "stoneness" (a quality noun).
    • drona gere (dron- + -a [Noun-class ending] + ger- "to think" + -e [Noun-class ending]) = "a stone in thought" (an abstract noun).
  • The verb omi- ("to be" — abstract/conditional/desiderative):

    Construction: omi- (past or present tense) + the participle of the main verb (in the appropriate tense). In the past tense, omi- expresses a conditional/unreal (missed) possibility; in the present tense, it expresses a desired possibility.

    Examples: omirazy aververi — I would have said (under a possible/impossible condition); ominazy aververi — I would say (if I wanted to).
  • Converbial complement (not to be confused with the "converbial adverbial"):

    Construction: BS + imperative form of the TMVA suffix + adverbial ending (e.g., BS + -ed-/-de- + -ov/-go).

    Semantics: identical in meaning to an action noun in direct-object position.

    Example: visevra lenedego ("to begin doing") = visevra yn lenejes.
  • Name of the language:

    Regla o Givelygan = Regl- (speech) + -a (Noun-class ending, Class II, singular, topic) + o (possession) + Gively- (ethnonym/toponym) + -gan (Noun-class ending, Class III, general number, topic) — "The language of the people/place of Givelu."

  • Development of the postpositional clitic -du:

    -du, as a universal accusative marker, developed by analogy from the "converbial complement" formed from a consonant-final BS, since it always ends in -d-ov [dɒw] regardless of voice. -d-ov subsequently shifted to [du] (e.g., defladov → defladu), and by this analogy -du spread to all parts of speech capable of taking an accusative form.

    It became a clitic attached with a hyphen after the word's ending, nearly displacing the archaic preposition yn (which survives in literary speech). Because it now sounds identical to the noun accusative (defla-du), the old form defladu ceased to be productive, leaving the archaic defladov and the new construction deflana-du.
